0. Project Objectives
Purpose
FixIt exists because finding reliable local help for small jobs is frustrating for everyone involved. People who need work done waste time asking around and often end up with someone unreliable. People who have the skills to do the work have no good way to find consistent clients beyond word of mouth. FixIt brings both sides together in one simple platform, creating economic opportunity for skilled workers while giving the rest of the community a trustworthy place to find help.
SMART Objectives
Objective 1: Build and deploy a functional web MVP of FixIt that allows users to register, post jobs, and receive offers from service providers — completed by the end of Stage 4 of the Holberton portfolio project timeline.
Objective 2: Design the platform so that a first-time user can post a job or create a service provider profile in under five minutes, measured by testing the flow with at least three people outside the team before final submission.
Objective 3: Document the full development process across all five stages so that any new team member or reviewer can understand what was built, why decisions were made, and how the system works — with all documentation submitted on time at each stage deadline.

Stakeholders

2. Project Scope
In-Scope
The MVP will include user registration and login for both job posters and service providers. It will have a job posting form where users can describe the task, set a location, choose a category, and suggest a budget. Service providers will have a browsable feed where they can see open jobs and send an offer or express interest. Once matched, both users will be able to message each other through a basic in-app chat. After the job is completed, both sides can leave a rating and a short review. The interface will be built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and the backend will be built with Python.
Out-of-Scope
The MVP will not include a payment system. Users will handle payment between themselves outside the platform. There will be no identity verification or background check system at this stage. We are not building a mobile app, only a web platform. Push notifications, advanced search filters, and any kind of AI-based job matching are also out of scope for this version.

3. Risks
Risk 1: The cold start problem
A marketplace only works when there are people on both sides. If we launch and only job posters sign up but no service providers do, or the other way around, the platform is useless. To manage this we plan to start in a very small, focused area and personally reach out to people we know on both sides to get the first few users going.
Risk 2: Trust and safety
People are inviting someone they found online into their home. If something goes wrong between a job poster and a service provider, it reflects badly on the platform. Our mitigation is to make the review and rating system a core part of the experience from day one, not an afterthought. Both users rate each other after every job, which creates accountability on both sides.
Risk 3: Scope creep
It is easy to keep adding features and never finish anything. We have already decided what is out of scope and we plan to stick to it. If anyone on the team wants to add something new, we agree to discuss it as a group first and only add it if we can do so without pushing back a deadline.
Risk 4: Uneven workload
With three people on a project it is easy for one person to end up doing most of the work, especially when schedules do not line up. We handle this by checking in twice a week and being upfront when someone is overloaded. If tasks are piling up on one person we redistribute before it becomes a problem.
Risk 5: Technical gaps
There will definitely be moments where none of us knows how to do something we need to build. Our plan is to use official documentation, ask our instructor, and give ourselves buffer time in the timeline so that learning something new does not automatically mean missing a deadline.

4. High-Level Project Plan
Here is how the project breaks down across all five stages:
Stage 1: Team Formation and Idea Development (Completed)
We formed the team, brainstormed eight ideas, evaluated them using a scoring rubric, and selected FixIt as our MVP. The Stage 1 report documents all of this in detail.
Stage 2: Project Charter Development (Current)
We are defining the project formally, putting down our objectives, scope, risks, stakeholders, and timeline in writing so that everyone is aligned before we start building anything.
Stage 3: Technical Documentation
We will map out the architecture of the application, define the database structure, describe the main features technically, and plan how the frontend and backend will communicate. This stage produces the blueprint we will actually build from.
Stage 4: MVP Development
This is where we build it. We will work feature by feature starting with user registration and job posting, then adding the offer system, messaging, and reviews. We will test as we go and do a final round of testing before submission.
Stage 5: Project Closure
We will present the finished MVP, write a final reflection on what we built, what worked, what did not, and what we would do differently. We will also make sure all documentation across every stage is complete and accessible.
Approximate Timeline
Weeks 1 and 2 covered team formation and idea selection, which is now complete. Weeks 3 and 4 are for the project charter, which is what we are finishing now. Weeks 5 and 6 will be used for technical documentation. Weeks 7 through 10 are reserved for actually building the MVP, which is the longest phase and the one that will require the most focused effort from all three of us. Weeks 11 and 12 are for final testing, the presentation, and project closure.

5. Summary
This charter is the agreement our team is working from. It lays out what we are building, who it is for, what we are not building, what could go wrong and how we plan to handle it, and when everything is supposed to happen. Every decision we make from here on should connect back to something in this document. If it does not, we probably need to talk about it as a team before moving forward.
